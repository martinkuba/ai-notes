# George R.R. Martin and other authors sue OpenAI for copyright infringement

**Original source:** [George R.R. Martin and other authors sue OpenAI for copyright infringement](https://www.theverge.com/2023/9/20/23882140/george-r-r-martin-lawsuit-openai-copyright-infringement)
**Author:** Emilia David

## Summary

The Authors Guild and 17 prominent authors, including George R.R. Martin, John Grisham, Jonathan Franzen, and Jodi Picoult, filed a lawsuit against OpenAI in the Southern District of New York, alleging copyright infringement. The complaint claims OpenAI copied their works wholesale, without permission or compensation, to train its large language models. The plaintiffs sought class action status for the case.

The lawsuit argues that OpenAI's LLMs threaten fiction writers' livelihoods by enabling anyone to generate text cheaply that they would otherwise pay writers to create. The authors contend the models could produce derivative works that mimic, summarize, or paraphrase their books, harming their market. They also assert that OpenAI could have trained on public domain works instead of copyrighted material without paying licensing fees.

This filing was part of a broader wave of legal action against generative AI companies over copyright issues. Earlier lawsuits by Michael Chabon, Sarah Silverman, Paul Tremblay, and others had already targeted OpenAI and Meta. Similar copyright disputes also extended to AI image generation platforms. In response to the growing legal landscape, Microsoft announced it would assume legal responsibility if commercial users of its Copilot AI service were sued over copyright issues.

## Main Ideas

- The Authors Guild and 17 well-known authors sued OpenAI for using copyrighted books to train LLMs without permission or payment.
- The plaintiffs sought class action status, arguing that generative AI threatens writers' ability to earn a living by enabling cheap automated text generation.
- The complaint noted that OpenAI could have used public domain works instead of copyrighted material for training.
- This lawsuit was part of a growing trend of copyright litigation against generative AI companies, including multiple prior suits against OpenAI and Meta.
- Microsoft responded to the broader copyright landscape by pledging to defend commercial users of its AI services from copyright lawsuits.

## Key Quotes

- "These authors' livelihoods derive from the works they create. But the Defendant's LLMs endanger fiction writers' ability to make a living in that the LLMs allow anyone to generate -- automatically and freely (or very cheaply) -- text that they would otherwise pay writers to create."
