# Behind the Curtain — America's next class war: AI fluency

**Original source:** [Behind the Curtain — America's next class war: AI fluency](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/24/ai-use-inequality-class)
**Author:** Jim VandeHei, Mike Allen

## Summary

Anthropic released its most granular data yet on AI usage patterns, revealing an emerging class divide based on AI fluency rather than simply whether people use AI or not. The "Anthropic Economic Index: Learning Curves" report studied over 1 million Claude conversations and found that experienced AI users get meaningfully better results than newcomers—a 10% higher success rate for those who've used Claude for six months or more—and this gap isn't explained by task type, location, or model choice. The real divide is between skilled AI users who leverage it as a broad "toolbox" for augmentation and newcomers who use it as a simple search engine.

The data shows a skills gap hardening into a class gap in real time. Two workflow categories—automated sales/outreach and automated trading—doubled in prevalence between November and February, threatening those jobs with displacement. Meanwhile, Washington D.C.'s Claude adoption rate is four times what you'd expect for a city its size, and global usage inequality has persisted across higher-income countries. An NBC News poll found 57% of voters believe AI's risks outweigh benefits, and only 26% have positive feelings about the technology.

The report arrives during an anxious period for AI and jobs, yet Washington is debating AI in the abstract—regulation, China competition, superintelligence—rather than confronting the concrete near-term problem of an emerging two-tier workforce. Neither political party has a plan for people on the wrong side of the AI fluency divide. The authors argue that the gap is escapable through experimentation and practice, urging people to move beyond using LLMs as search engines toward using them as thought partners, idea stress-testers, and business planners.

## Main Ideas

- Experienced AI users (6+ months) have a 10% higher success rate than newcomers, creating a skills-based class divide
- The real divide isn't between AI users and non-users—it's between sophisticated and casual users
- Automated sales/outreach and automated trading workflows doubled in prevalence in just three months
- Washington D.C. has 4x the expected Claude adoption rate for its population size
- 57% of voters believe AI's risks outweigh benefits; only 26% view the technology positively
- Neither political party has a plan for the emerging two-tier workforce

## Key Quotes

- "Much of the discussion focuses on how AI is something that happens *to* you. This analysis shows you can develop skills that make you better at getting value out of Claude." — Peter McCrory, Anthropic's head of economics
