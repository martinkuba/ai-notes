# Robot takeover? Not quite. Here's what AI doomsday would look like

**Original source:** [Robot takeover? Not quite. Here's what AI doomsday would look like](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/jun/03/ai-danger-doomsday-chatgpt-robots-fears)
**Author:** Kari Paul

## Summary

This Guardian article examines what AI-related societal harm actually looks like, contrasting sci-fi imagery of robot takeovers with the more realistic and insidious threats that experts identify. Rather than a sudden catastrophic event, experts describe a "creeping deterioration" of foundational societal structures. The piece was written during the 2023 wave of AI alarm, when over 300 industry leaders signed a letter comparing AI risk to pandemics and nuclear war.

Experts interviewed identify three primary areas of concern. First, the erosion of shared truth through AI-amplified misinformation and disinformation, with LLMs prone to hallucination and capable of being weaponized to spread falsehoods at scale. Peter Wang of Anaconda argues that social media's recommendation algorithms were humanity's "first encounter with really dumb AI" and we "utterly failed that encounter," warning that future models trained on AI-generated misinformation could create a "model cannibalism" effect. Second, the manipulation of users through human-like chatbot interactions, noting tragic incidents where chatbots encouraged harmful behavior. Third, an impending labor crisis, with studies suggesting AI could replace 85 million to 300 million jobs. Despite these concerns, experts note that AI also holds enormous positive potential, though regulation has been virtually absent.

## Main Ideas

- AI "doomsday" is more likely a slow societal erosion than a dramatic robot takeover
- Disinformation amplified by AI threatens shared understanding of truth, with risks of political unrest and manipulation
- Chatbots' conversational, human-like nature makes users more susceptible to manipulation and trust
- Mass job displacement could create economic instability and political unrest with no existing safety framework
- The tech industry's competitive "arms race" to deploy AI is accelerating risks faster than regulation can respond
- Social media algorithms were an early test of managing AI-like technology, and society largely failed that test
