# IIIa. Racing to the Trillion-Dollar Cluster

**Original source:** [IIIa. Racing to the Trillion-Dollar Cluster](https://situational-awareness.ai/racing-to-the-trillion-dollar-cluster/)
**Author:** SITUATIONAL AWARENESS - The Decade Ahead

## Summary

This essay lays out the extraordinary scale of capital investment and industrial mobilization required to build the compute infrastructure for AGI and superintelligence. Following a trend of roughly 0.5 orders of magnitude per year in training compute growth, the author projects that individual training clusters will reach hundreds of billions of dollars by 2028 and a trillion dollars by the end of the decade. The trillion-dollar cluster would require approximately 100 gigawatts of power -- over 20% of total US electricity production -- and represent one of the largest capital buildouts in history.

The essay argues this trajectory is economically justified. AI revenue is growing rapidly, with companies like OpenAI doubling revenue roughly every six months. The author projects big tech companies could hit $100B annual AI revenue run rates by around 2026, which would unleash massive further investment. Historical precedents -- from British railway investments (40% of GDP over a decade) to wartime borrowing (over 100% of GDP) -- demonstrate that comparable levels of capital mobilization have occurred before for transformative technologies and existential threats.

On the supply side, the author identifies power as the single biggest constraint, not chips. While GPU production can scale relatively quickly (with TSMC expanding fab capacity), securing tens of gigawatts of power requires years of lead time. The essay argues forcefully that the US must use natural gas and pursue aggressive deregulation to meet power demands, warning that climate commitments and regulatory barriers risk driving AGI datacenters to Middle Eastern autocracies. The author frames this as a national security imperative: the clusters being built today may be the ones on which AGI and superintelligence are trained, and having that infrastructure under the control of foreign dictatorships would create irreversible security risks.

## Main Ideas

- Training compute is growing at ~0.5 OOMs/year, pointing toward individual $100B+ clusters by 2028 and $1T clusters by ~2030.
- Total annual AI investment could exceed $1T by 2027, driven by rapidly growing AI revenue and the expectation of AGI-level returns.
- Power, not chips, is the binding constraint: the trillion-dollar cluster would need ~100GW, equivalent to over 20% of US electricity production, requiring tens of new power plants.
- The US has abundant natural gas to solve the power problem, but climate commitments, permitting, and regulatory barriers stand in the way.
- Historical precedents (railways, telecoms, wartime mobilization) show that capital buildouts of this magnitude have been achieved before.
- Building AGI infrastructure in the US rather than the Middle East is framed as a critical national security priority to prevent adversaries from controlling or seizing the clusters.
- Chip supply (TSMC fab expansion, CoWoS packaging, HBM memory) will require massive investment but is ultimately solvable.
