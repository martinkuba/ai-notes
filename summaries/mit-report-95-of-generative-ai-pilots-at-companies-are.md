# MIT report: 95% of generative AI pilots at companies are failing

**Original source:** [MIT report: 95% of generative AI pilots at companies are failing](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)
**Author:** Sheryl Estrada

## Summary

A new report from MIT's NANDA initiative, titled "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025," reveals that approximately 95% of enterprise generative AI pilot programs are failing to achieve rapid revenue acceleration. The research is based on 150 interviews with leaders, a survey of 350 employees, and analysis of 300 public AI deployments. While a small minority of companies — particularly nimble startups — are excelling with generative AI, the vast majority of enterprise implementations are stalling with little measurable P&L impact.

The core problem identified is not AI model quality but rather a "learning gap" in enterprise integration. Generic tools like ChatGPT work well for individuals because of their flexibility, but enterprise deployments stall because they fail to learn from or adapt to organizational workflows. The report also reveals a significant misalignment in how companies allocate AI budgets: more than half of spending goes to sales and marketing tools, yet the biggest ROI comes from back-office automation — eliminating business process outsourcing, cutting external agency costs, and streamlining operations.

The report finds that how companies adopt AI matters enormously. Purchasing AI tools from specialized vendors and building partnerships succeed about 67% of the time, while internal builds succeed only about one-third as often. The study also highlights widespread "shadow AI" usage — employees using unsanctioned tools like ChatGPT for work — and notes that workforce disruption is already underway, particularly in customer support and administrative roles, though companies are handling this through attrition rather than mass layoffs. The most advanced organizations are beginning to experiment with agentic AI systems that can learn, remember, and act independently.

## Main Ideas

- About 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots fail to deliver rapid revenue acceleration, despite massive corporate investment.
- The root cause of failure is not AI model quality but flawed enterprise integration — tools that don't learn from feedback or adapt to workflows.
- Over half of AI budgets go to sales and marketing, but the highest ROI comes from back-office automation (BPO elimination, agency cost reduction, operations streamlining).
- Buying AI from specialized vendors succeeds ~67% of the time versus ~22% for internal builds, challenging the trend of companies building proprietary AI systems.
- Widespread "shadow AI" usage by employees with personal tools like ChatGPT signals that individual adoption is far ahead of corporate deployment.
- Workforce disruption is concentrated in previously outsourced, low-value roles, with companies opting for attrition over layoffs.
- Empowering line managers (not just central AI labs) to drive adoption is a key success factor.

## Key Quotes

- "Some large companies' pilots and younger startups are really excelling with generative AI... they pick one pain point, execute well, and partner smartly with companies who use their tools."
- "Almost everywhere we went, enterprises were trying to build their own tool," but the data showed purchased solutions delivered more reliable results.
