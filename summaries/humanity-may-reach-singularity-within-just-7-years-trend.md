# Humanity May Reach Singularity Within Just 7 Years, Trend Shows

**Original source:** [Humanity May Reach Singularity Within Just 7 Years, Trend Shows](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/humanity-may-reach-singularity-within-222900100.html)
**Author:** Darren Orf

## Summary

This article reports on a novel metric for measuring progress toward technological singularity, developed by Translated, a Rome-based translation company. The metric, called "Time to Edit" (TTE), measures how long professional human editors take to correct AI-generated translations compared to human-generated ones. Over an eight-year period from 2014 to 2022, analyzing over two billion post-edits, Translated's AI showed slow but undeniable improvement in closing the gap toward human-level translation quality.

CEO Marco Trombetti presented these findings at a conference in Orlando, Florida, arguing that language translation is one of the most challenging tasks for AI because language is "the most natural thing for humans." He suggested that an AI capable of translating speech at human-level accuracy could show signs of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), and that by this metric, singularity could be approached by the end of the decade. However, the article notes important caveats: perfecting human speech translation does not necessarily make a machine intelligent, and researchers continue to debate what "intelligence" actually means.

## Main Ideas

- Translated developed the "Time to Edit" (TTE) metric to quantify AI progress toward human-level translation quality.
- Over 8 years and 2 billion post-edits, the gap between AI and human translation quality has been steadily closing.
- Language translation is considered one of the hardest AI challenges because language is deeply natural to humans.
- By this specific metric, singularity could be approached by the end of the 2020s decade.
- The article acknowledges limitations: achieving human-level translation does not necessarily equate to true machine intelligence or AGI.

## Key Quotes

- "That's because language is the most natural thing for humans. Nonetheless, the data Translated collected clearly shows that machines are not that far from closing the gap." -- Marco Trombetti
- "The change is so small that every single day you don't perceive it, but when you see progress across 10 years, that is impressive." -- Marco Trombetti
