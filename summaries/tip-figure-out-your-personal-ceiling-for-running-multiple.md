# Tip: Figure out your personal ceiling for running multiple agents in parallel

**Original source:** [Tip: Figure out your personal ceiling for running multiple agents...](https://x.com/addyosmani/status/2040132221328388418/?rw_tt_thread=True)
**Author:** Addy Osmani

## Summary

Addy Osmani highlights an under-discussed aspect of working with AI coding agents: the cognitive cost to the human in the loop. While the dominant narrative focuses on throughput and parallelism — running more agents simultaneously — Osmani argues that more agents running doesn't mean more of the human is available. Running multiple agents in parallel requires holding multiple problem contexts simultaneously, making continuous judgment calls, and absorbing the anxiety of not knowing what any given agent might be getting wrong. This represents a new kind of cognitive labor that we don't yet have good language for.

Osmani shares that he now treats long agentic sessions like deep focus work: time-boxed with tighter scopes per agent to reduce mental overhead. He argues that finding your personal ceiling with these tools is itself a skill, and most people will learn it the hard way before learning it intentionally. The thread includes quotes from Simon Willison (via Lenny Rachitsky's podcast) who describes being "wiped out" by 11am after running four parallel agents, despite 25 years of software engineering experience. Willison emphasizes that there's a limit on human cognition — how much you can hold in your head at once — and we need to find responsible ways to use these tools without burning out.

## Main Ideas

- Running multiple AI agents in parallel creates a new, under-discussed form of cognitive labor for the human operator
- More agents running doesn't mean more human capacity is available — context-switching and judgment calls are the bottleneck
- Time-boxing agentic sessions and tightening scope per agent reduces mental overhead
- Finding your personal ceiling for parallel agent work is itself a skill that must be learned
- Even experienced engineers (25+ years) report exhaustion from parallel agent management by midday
- The industry conversation about AI productivity overlooks the human cognitive cost

## Key Quotes

- "Using coding agents well is taking every inch of my 25 years of experience as a software engineer, and it is mentally exhausting." — Simon Willison
- "There is a limit on human cognition. Even if you're not reviewing everything they're doing, how much you can hold in your head at one time." — Simon Willison
