# The Effects Of GenAI On Learning Performance: A Meta-Analysis Study

**Original source:** [The Effects Of GenAI On Learning Performance: A Meta-Analysis Study](https://readwise.io/reader/document_raw_content/451281747)
**Author:** Seyfullah Gökçül, Fatih Erdoğan

## Summary

This meta-analysis study synthesized 31 experimental and quasi-experimental studies involving 2,646 participants to determine the overall effect of Generative AI (GenAI) on learning performance. The results demonstrate that GenAI-supported instruction has a **moderately positive effect** on learning performance (Hedges' g = .689), representing a medium effect size according to Cohen's classification. The study found no publication bias, indicating robust results.

The research examined seven moderator variables to understand what factors influence GenAI's effectiveness: sample level, sample size, research design, learning domain, research setting, intervention duration, GenAI tool type, and testing format. Three moderators significantly influenced effectiveness: intervention duration (with 1-3 month interventions showing the strongest effect), GenAI tool type (GenAI-supported systems outperformed simple chatbots), and testing format (achievement tests were more sensitive to GenAI's impact than knowledge or performance tests). Notable findings include that K-12 students showed larger effect sizes than undergraduate students, flipped learning environments were most conducive to GenAI integration, and smaller sample sizes (1-100 participants) demonstrated larger effects than larger groups.

The meta-analysis reveals important contextual factors for successful GenAI implementation in education while acknowledging heterogeneity in study designs and implementation approaches.

## Main Ideas

- GenAI demonstrates a medium positive effect (g = .689) on overall learning performance, comparable to effect sizes in other educational technology meta-analyses
- **K-12 education shows the largest effect sizes** (g = 0.993), suggesting younger students benefit most from GenAI integration, likely due to greater technological adaptability
- **Intervention duration matters significantly**: 1-3 month interventions are optimal; longer interventions (>3 months) showed negative effects, possibly due to novelty wear-off or implementation challenges
- **GenAI-supported systems substantially outperform chatbots** (g = 1.047 vs. 0.357), indicating that comprehensive AI-integrated systems are more effective than conversational interfaces alone
- **Achievement tests capture GenAI's benefits better** than knowledge or performance tests, suggesting GenAI particularly enhances higher-order thinking and complex skill assessment
- **Flipped learning environments maximize GenAI effectiveness** (g = 1.818), while blended learning showed negative effects, highlighting the importance of pedagogical integration strategy
- **Smaller groups benefit more from GenAI** (1-100 participants showed larger effects than 100+ participants), indicating personalization and feedback mechanisms work better at scale

## Key Quotes

- "GenAI has the remarkable potential to revolutionize learning and information accessibility by adjusting to individual preferences and requirements, thus making education more accessible and compelling for all learners."

- "The fact that studies at the K-12 level had a larger effect size suggests that GenAI can positively affect learning performance, especially at this level of education. This may be explained by the fact that younger students use technology more creatively and inspiringly."

- "Flipped learning environments highly influence the successful integration of GenAI tools into learning performance...these environments encourage students to participate more actively and direct their learning processes."
