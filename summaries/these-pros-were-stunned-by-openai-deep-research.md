# These pros were stunned by OpenAI Deep Research

**Original source:** [These pros were stunned by OpenAI Deep Research](https://www.understandingai.org/p/these-experts-were-stunned-by-openai)
**Author:** Timothy B. Lee

## Summary

This article reports on a rigorous real-world evaluation of OpenAI's Deep Research product, which uses a variant of the o3 reasoning model capable of thinking for up to 30 minutes and iteratively searching the web. The author solicited difficult domain-specific questions from 19 professionals across fields including architecture, law, mechanical engineering, teaching, and medical research, then compared OpenAI's responses against Google's competing Deep Research product in a blind test.

The results were striking: 16 out of 19 participants preferred OpenAI's responses, and 7 of 19 rated them at or near the level of an experienced professional. A retired architect said a 15,000-word building code checklist was "better than intern work" and would take 6-8 hours of human labor; an antitrust lawyer said an 8,000-word legal report "compares favorably with an entry-level attorney" and would take 15-20 hours to compile; a mechanical engineer estimated his report would take a professional a full week to produce. The majority of respondents estimated at least 10 hours of human labor for comparable output.

The article argues that Deep Research's success has broader implications beyond a single product. It demonstrates that reinforcement learning techniques used for reasoning models generalize well beyond math and programming into "softer" domains like law and architecture. It also points toward a better paradigm for RAG (retrieval augmented generation) applications, where iterative search replaces single-pass document retrieval. The author concludes that the current AI paradigm has more headroom for improvement than many expected, driven by the ability of reasoning models to "teach themselves" through self-play and iterative training.

## Main Ideas

- OpenAI Deep Research was preferred over Google's version by 16 of 19 professional evaluators in a blind test
- Seven professionals rated OpenAI's output at or near experienced-professional level
- Deep Research uses iterative web searching, refining queries based on what it learns — similar to human research behavior
- Reinforcement learning techniques for reasoning generalize well beyond math/code to law, architecture, and medicine
- Deep Research points toward a better RAG paradigm: iterative retrieval instead of single-pass search
- The success suggests significant remaining headroom for AI capability improvement through self-play training
