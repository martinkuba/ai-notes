# AI Promise and Chip Precariousness

**Original source:** [AI Promise and Chip Precariousness](https://stratechery.com/2025/ai-promise-and-chip-precariousness/)
**Author:** Ben Thompson

## Summary

Ben Thompson surveys the competitive landscape of frontier AI models in early 2025 and then pivots to the geopolitical precariousness of the semiconductor supply chain that underpins the entire AI industry. He assesses the major players: Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 3.7 excels through post-training refinement, xAI's Grok 3 demonstrates the brute-force benefits of massive compute, OpenAI's ChatGPT maintains the strongest consumer brand with 400 million weekly users, and DeepSeek has proven that clever engineering can rival models built with far more resources. Thompson argues that this competitive ecosystem is healthy for AI's development, with private funding pursuing the frontier while competition drives API prices down.

However, the article's core argument is that the entire U.S. AI ecosystem rests on a dangerously fragile foundation: near-total dependence on TSMC in Taiwan. Thompson traces how U.S. chip export bans on China have paradoxically reduced China's dependence on TSMC (by forcing development of alternatives like Huawei's Ascend chips on SMIC), while the U.S. has become more dependent on Taiwan than ever. He argues that the Trump administration's proposal to have TSMC take over Intel's foundries is impractical because foundry processes are not transferable, and it would not solve the deeper strategic vulnerability.

Thompson proposes a controversial five-part plan: (1) end the China chip ban to restore China's dependency on TSMC and thus Taiwan, (2) double down on semiconductor equipment bans to limit China's domestic fab capabilities, (3) subsidize trailing-edge fab construction in the U.S. for national security, (4) engineer an Intel foundry spin-off with guaranteed customers and subsidized Nvidia orders, and (5) indemnify AI models trained on U.S.-made chips against copyright claims. The overarching strategy is to buy time by making Taiwan indispensable to China while building domestic U.S. chip capacity.

## Main Ideas

- The AI model landscape is competitively healthy, with different labs (Anthropic, xAI, OpenAI, DeepSeek) excelling through different strategies: post-training refinement, raw compute, consumer product polish, and engineering efficiency respectively.
- OpenAI's biggest asset is the ChatGPT brand and consumer mindshare, but it has moved too slowly on an ad-supported tier that would maximize its addressable market.
- DeepSeek's open-source models set the floor for "minimum open capability," making it hard to justify closed models that perform at or below that level.
- U.S. chip export bans have backfired strategically by reducing China's dependence on TSMC while the U.S. AI economy has become more dependent on Taiwan.
- TSMC's dominance extends beyond leading-edge chips to trailing-edge capacity that is equally critical and even harder to replace domestically.
- Thompson's proposed solution involves the counterintuitive step of lifting the China chip ban to restore Taiwan's deterrent value, while simultaneously cutting off semiconductor equipment sales to limit China's domestic fab growth.

## Key Quotes

- "We have created a situation where China is less dependent on Taiwan, even while we are more dependent on the island."
- "AI is in an exciting place; it's also a very precarious one."
