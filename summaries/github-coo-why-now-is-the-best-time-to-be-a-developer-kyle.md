# GitHub COO: Why Now Is the BEST Time to Be a Developer | Kyle Diagle

**Original source:** [GitHub COO: Why Now Is the BEST Time to Be a Developer | Kyle Diagle](https://youtube.com/watch?v=cMubtUs8dvs&si=yPwbdu3_wnBmi_TA)
**Author:** Sajjaad Khader

## Summary

Kyle Daigle, Chief Operating Officer of GitHub, discusses why this is an unprecedented time for software developers despite concerns about AI disruption. He explains that the industry is transitioning from AI code completion to "agentic AI"—autonomous agents that can break down complex tasks into components and execute them independently. Rather than replacing developers, AI represents a shift to higher levels of abstraction, similar to historical transitions from punch cards to assembly to high-level languages, each resulting in more programmers rather than fewer.

Daigle emphasizes that developers remain essential because humans alone can define what problems need solving and understand the nuanced requirements of users. Even as AI agents handle more technical implementation, human judgment, creative problem-solving, and understanding of user needs remain irreplaceable. He points to concrete examples of interns leveraging AI tools to accomplish months of work in weeks, demonstrating that developers fluent in AI amplification gain significant competitive advantages.

The key takeaway is that learning to code remains worthwhile, but the most valuable skill going forward is understanding how to think about problems and leverage AI tools effectively. Technical depth matters less than comprehending why certain tools and approaches were chosen, as this demonstrates the problem-solving mindset employers genuinely seek.

## Main Ideas

- **Agentic AI represents the next frontier**: AI is evolving from code completion to autonomous agents that can break complex tasks into subtasks, make tool calls, and implement solutions end-to-end
- **Historical precedent suggests growth, not decline**: Every major technology shift (punch cards → assembly → high-level languages) resulted in more jobs, not fewer, as developers could tackle increasingly complex problems
- **Human judgment remains irreplaceable**: Someone must define what to build and validate solutions match user needs; AI cannot replicate this creative spark and understanding of incentives
- **Learning to code is still valuable**: AI accelerates the learning process by letting students build complete projects faster, then explore underlying code to understand concepts
- **The hiring focus is shifting**: Employers seek developers who understand how and why they solved problems with particular tools and approaches, not just those who know syntax
- **AI amplification creates immediate career advantages**: Developers who master AI tools can accomplish in weeks what previously took months, creating stark differentiation in job markets
- **Bigger problems become solvable**: AI enables previously economically unviable solutions, expanding the total addressable problem space rather than shrinking it

## Key Quotes

> "AI is letting us use an even higher level of abstraction. I'm able to do even more without having to work into the nitty-gritty details, and then I can go solve more problems."

> "Every single time these moments of transformation have happened, whether it be in industry or in software, there are more people that are needed to do the work on the other side."

> "Someone still has to tell it what to do... I need to understand who you are, what you care about... Is this going to solve your problem? That's something that humans, I think, are the only ones that are able to do."
