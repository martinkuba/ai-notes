# Health care AI, intended to save money, turns out to require a lot of expensive humans

**Original source:** [Health care AI, intended to save money, turns out to require a lot of expensive humans](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/health-care-ai-cost-humans/)
**Author:** Darius Tahir

## Summary

Healthcare AI systems, despite their promise to reduce costs and improve care, require substantial ongoing human oversight and resources to function reliably. The article opens with a case at the University of Pennsylvania Health System, where an AI algorithm predicting patient mortality decayed by 7 percentage points during the COVID-19 pandemic, failing hundreds of times to prompt doctors to initiate critical end-of-life discussions. The lead researcher believes many such algorithms degraded during the pandemic, and most institutions are not routinely monitoring their performance.

The core dilemma is that AI systems in healthcare need consistent monitoring, staffing, and infrastructure to remain effective. Stanford Health Care's chief data scientist raised the concern that if AI increases care costs by 20%, its viability is questionable. The FDA commissioner stated he does not believe any US health system is capable of properly validating an AI algorithm in clinical use. Standards for evaluating and comparing AI products are largely nonexistent -- there is no Consumer Reports for healthcare AI, and even basic performance benchmarks are missing.

Specific failures illustrate the scope of the problem. Stanford researchers found that LLMs summarizing patient histories had a 35% error rate even in the best case. A Mass General Brigham team discovered that a genetic counseling tool exhibited "nondeterminism," giving different answers to the same question. Auditing just two models for fairness at Stanford required 8-10 months and 115 person-hours. Experts floated the idea of using AI to monitor AI, but acknowledged this would require even more expensive specialists -- creating a recursive cost problem that challenges the fundamental economic premise of healthcare AI.

## Main Ideas

- Healthcare AI algorithms can silently degrade over time, as demonstrated by a Penn Medicine mortality prediction tool that worsened by 7 percentage points during COVID-19.
- Most healthcare institutions are not routinely monitoring the performance of deployed AI systems.
- There are no industry standards for evaluating, comparing, or monitoring healthcare AI products.
- The FDA commissioner stated no US health system is currently capable of properly validating clinical AI algorithms.
- LLMs summarizing patient medical histories showed a 35% error rate even in the best case at Stanford.
- Auditing AI models for fairness and reliability is extremely resource-intensive: 8-10 months and 115 person-hours for just two models at Stanford.
- The cost of human oversight needed to maintain AI systems may undermine the economic case for deploying them in healthcare.
