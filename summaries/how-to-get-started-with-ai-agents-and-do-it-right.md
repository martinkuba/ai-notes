# How to get started with AI agents (and do it right)

**Original source:** [How to get started with AI agents (and do it right)](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-to-get-started-with-ai-agents-and-do-it-right/)
**Author:** Taryn Plumb

## Summary

VentureBeat reports on the challenges enterprises face when building and deploying AI agents, drawing on expert analysis from Forrester, Databricks, and consulting firm Intelligence Briefing. The central warning is that nearly three-quarters of organizations attempting to build AI agents in-house will fail, according to Forrester predictions. The article identifies several root causes: AI agent architectures are far more complex than enterprises realize, requiring multiple models, advanced RAG stacks, sophisticated data architectures, and specialized expertise that most organizations lack. Retrieval augmented generation (RAG) and vector databases in particular are stumbling blocks -- getting accurate outputs in expected time frames, understanding re-ranking, and managing context window limitations all require deep technical knowledge.

The article offers practical guidance for enterprises evaluating their options (third-party, open-source, or custom-built agents). Experts recommend starting with targeted questions: where does the team spend most of its time, which tasks are most time-consuming, and how complex are they? Organizations should check existing software licenses for agent capabilities before building from scratch, and should focus on one business function initially. A cross-functional approach involving business leadership, software development, data science, and UX teams is essential for building an effective AI strategy. The article emphasizes that post-deployment maintenance is not optional -- all AI agent systems require ongoing tweaking, evaluation, and support to maintain and improve accuracy over time. Organizations with robust internal development teams and well-governed data can succeed with custom builds, but many will be most successful partnering with third-party providers who have the bandwidth to keep up with rapidly evolving architectures.

## Main Ideas

- Forrester predicts nearly 75% of organizations attempting to build AI agents in-house will fail due to the complexity of required architectures (multiple models, advanced RAG, specialized expertise).
- RAG systems are a common failure point: they take 6-8 weeks to build and optimize, with initial accuracy rates around 55% before iterative improvement.
- Enterprises should start with targeted questions about where teams spend time and which tasks are most complex before choosing build-vs-buy approaches.
- A cross-functional approach (business leaders, developers, data scientists, UX managers) is critical for successful AI agent strategy.
- Post-deployment maintenance is mandatory: all AI agent systems require ongoing support, tweaking, and adjustment -- there is "no free lunch post-deployment."
- Organizations with well-governed, documented, and tagged data are best positioned to build custom agents; those without should partner with third-party providers.
- The skills to build production AI agents have not yet been broadly developed and quantified, creating a talent gap across the industry.
