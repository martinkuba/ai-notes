# What I'm Hearing About Cognitive Debt (So Far)

**Original source:** [What I'm Hearing About Cognitive Debt (So Far)](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/18/cognitive-debt-revisited/)

## Summary

Margaret Storey explores how generative and agentic AI is amplifying "cognitive debt"—the growing gap between a system's evolving structure and a team's shared understanding of how and why it works. Drawing on discussions from Simon Willison, Martin Fowler, and others in the developer community, she identifies a consistent pattern: velocity outpaces understanding. While teams can ship faster with AI assistance, developers report losing confidence in making changes, facing heavier review burdens, and experiencing debugging friction and onboarding delays. Cognitive debt is fundamentally different from technical debt because it lives in people, not code—manifesting as stress, fatigue, and erosion of collective mental models.

Storey argues that repaying cognitive debt requires maintaining a distributed theory of the system across multiple channels: people, documentation, tests, conversations, tooling, and AI agents. The challenge intensifies as AI lowers the cost of producing new code and structure, making it easier for architecture to evolve faster than understanding can stabilize. Even well-disciplined teams must consciously shape their practices to keep understanding aligned with change. She notes encouraging signs that teams are actively mitigating cognitive debt through rigorous code reviews, tests that capture intent, continuously updated design documents, and deliberately using AI to make cognitive work more visible rather than obscure it.

The core insight is that as AI reduces technical friction, **shared understanding may become the bottleneck on performance**. The question for high-performing teams is how they will shape socio-technical practices and tools to externalize intent and sustain collective theory—not just accelerate code production.

## Main Ideas

- **Cognitive debt** is the gap between a system's structure and a team's shared understanding of how and why it works; AI acceleration amplifies this problem by lowering the cost of producing code faster than understanding can stabilize
- Velocity can outpace understanding: teams ship faster but lose confidence in making changes, face heavier review burdens, slower onboarding, and debugging friction
- Cognitive debt lives in people, not code; it manifests as stress, fatigue, and erosion of mental models, affecting developer wellbeing and effectiveness beyond software quality
- Repaying cognitive debt requires maintaining a distributed theory across people, documentation, tests, conversations, tooling, and AI agents—not just code refactoring or architecture updates
- Emerging mitigation strategies include rigorous review practices, tests that capture intent, continuous documentation updates, treating prototypes as disposable, and deliberately using AI to make cognitive work visible
- Shared understanding and collective mental models may become the primary performance bottleneck as AI reduces technical friction
- Success depends on how teams shape socio-technical practices to externalize intent and maintain coherent theories of their systems
