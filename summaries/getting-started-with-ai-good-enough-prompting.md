# Getting started with AI: Good enough prompting

**Original source:** [Getting started with AI: Good enough prompting](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/getting-started-with-ai-good-enough)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Ethan Mollick argues that most people do not need to learn formal prompt engineering to benefit from AI. He opens with a study showing that doctors using GPT-4 for diagnosis performed no better than those without AI, partly due to algorithmic aversion and partly because they treated the AI like a search engine rather than giving it full context. This pattern of underutilization is widespread: most people have tried AI but have not invested the roughly 10 hours needed to develop real intuition for its capabilities.

Mollick proposes a framework of "good enough prompting" split into two categories. For task prompting, he suggests treating AI like an infinitely patient new coworker who forgets everything between conversations. Key principles include: using AI in areas of your expertise so you can spot errors, being very specific about what you want, providing context and examples, giving feedback iteratively, and leveraging the AI's infinite patience to request abundance (ask for 30 ideas instead of 5, request multiple drafts in different tones). For thinking prompting, Mollick recommends simply having a natural dialogue with the AI, using it as a rubber duck for working through problems or as a genuine thinking partner that can provide strategic and entrepreneurial advice.

The central message is pragmatic: formal prompt engineering matters for production systems and scaled applications, but for individual users exploring AI, the most important step is simply to start using it consistently. Spending 10 hours with a frontier model on tasks that matter to you will build natural intuition for effective prompting far better than studying techniques in the abstract.

## Main Ideas

- Doctors using AI for diagnosis performed no better than those without it, largely because they used it like a search engine instead of providing full context.
- Most people need about 10 hours of hands-on AI use to develop genuine intuition for what it can do.
- Formal prompt engineering is unnecessary for most individual users; "good enough" prompting suffices for personal exploration.
- Treat AI like an infinitely patient new coworker: be specific, provide context, use it in your area of expertise, and iterate through dialogue.
- Leverage AI's infinite patience by requesting abundance -- multiple drafts, dozens of ideas, varied tones -- rather than single outputs.
- AI can serve as a valuable thinking partner or rubber duck, even when its specific advice is imperfect.
- Newer, larger models are becoming less sensitive to prompting technique, making good-enough prompting increasingly viable.

## Key Quotes

- "They were treating [the AI] like a search engine for directed questions... It was only a fraction of the doctors who realized they could literally copy-paste in the entire case history into the chatbot and just ask it to give a comprehensive answer."
- "Your goal is simple: spend 10 hours using AI on tasks that actually matter to you. After that, you'll have a natural sense of how AI fits into your work and life."
