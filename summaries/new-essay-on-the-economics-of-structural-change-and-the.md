# New essay on the economics of structural change and the post-commodity future of work

**Original source:** [New essay on the economics of structural change and the...](https://x.com/alexolegimas/status/2044075001360306509/?rw_tt_thread=True)
**Author:** Alex Imas

## Summary

Alex Imas presents an economic analysis of how advanced AI will reshape labor markets and employment through the lens of scarcity and sectoral demand shifts. Rather than assuming mass unemployment, he argues that as AI automates commodity production, labor will reallocate to sectors with high income elasticity—specifically what he calls the "relational sector," where human involvement is intrinsic to the value proposition. Drawing on historical precedent (agriculture and manufacturing became less central to economies as they automated), Imas contends that human preferences have fundamentally mimetic and comparative dimensions rooted in status, exclusivity, and social desirability. These preferences are inherently non-satiated, meaning demand grows faster than income. His empirical work demonstrates that consumer willingness to pay roughly doubles when exclusivity is involved, and that human provenance significantly commands premiums over AI-generated alternatives. This framework suggests that while aggregate labor share might decline, labor will concentrate in sectors aligned with deep human desires for status and relational connection, maintaining a meaningful economic role for human workers in a post-AGI world.

## Main Ideas

- **Scarcity drives economics**: Understanding what remains scarce after AI automation determines how labor markets restructure and where employment concentrates
- **Structural shift, not job destruction**: Labor reallocates to high-income-elasticity sectors rather than disappearing; historical automation (agriculture, manufacturing) reduced employment share in those sectors while other sectors grew
- **Relational sectors absorb labor**: Jobs where human involvement is intrinsic to value (art, services, relational goods) exhibit high income elasticity and form the post-commodity economic core
- **Mimetic preferences drive demand**: Human desire has a comparative dimension—we value things partly because others want them or cannot have them, creating non-satiated demand that grows with wealth
- **Exclusivity and provenance matter**: Experimental evidence shows willingness to pay doubles with exclusivity, and human-made goods command 44% premiums over AI alternatives compared to only 21% for AI-made goods
- **Labor share distinction**: While aggregate labor share may decline, sectoral composition shifts toward relational work aligned with fundamental human preferences for status and connection
- **Income effects dominate structural change**: Research by Comin et al. suggests 75%+ of observed structural change results from income effects as people shift consumption patterns with rising wealth

## Key Quotes

"Almost any question about the impact of advanced AI on the economy needs to start at the same place: what is still scarce? Answer that, and the analysis becomes pretty straightforward."

"The ironic consequence: the sector that gets automated becomes a smaller share of the economy, not a larger one. Agriculture got massively more productive and its share of employment collapsed."

"Human desire has a mimetic, relational dimension. We don't just want things for their intrinsic properties. We want what others want, and we want it more when others can't have it."
