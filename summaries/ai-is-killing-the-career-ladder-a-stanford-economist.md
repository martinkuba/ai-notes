# AI Is Killing the Career Ladder. A Stanford Economist Explains What Comes Next

**Original source:** [AI Is Killing the Career Ladder. A Stanford Economist Explains What Comes Next | Bharat Chandar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FX0De_ccROU)
**Author:** EO

## Summary

Economist Barrett Shunder from Stanford Digital Economy Lab presents research showing that young workers in AI-exposed jobs (software development, customer service, administrative roles) are experiencing 16% slower employment growth compared to peers in less exposed roles. Importantly, this divergence appears primarily among early-career workers, with experienced workers maintaining employment growth trends. The key insight is that young workers typically perform implementation tasks that rely on book knowledge, while experienced workers leverage tacit knowledge, strategic thinking, and social interaction—precisely the areas where AI currently struggles most.

Rather than viewing AI purely as a substitute for human labor, Shunder advocates for a "career lattice" model where workers can more easily transition between professions through AI-enabled learning. He identifies three capabilities AI is unlikely to replace in the short to medium term: physical tasks (without robotics advances), strategic thinking and guidance, and social interaction. The central opportunity lies in using AI as an educational tool for augmentation rather than displacement. By leveraging AI to help workers acquire new skills faster and expand their task repertoires, we can shift the narrative from job elimination to capability expansion—enabling workers to focus on the distinctly human work of defining what needs to be done while AI handles implementation.

## Main Ideas

- Young workers in AI-exposed occupations are seeing 16% slower employment growth, suggesting potential structural labor market changes driven by AI capabilities
- AI excels at implementation tasks but struggles with tacit knowledge, strategic thinking, and social interaction—creating distinct advantages for experienced workers
- The distinction between "augmentation" (expanding task scope through education) and "substitution" (reducing tasks through automation) determines whether AI benefits or harms workers
- AI-as-educational-tool represents a major opportunity for creating a "career lattice" where workers can transition between professions more easily as labor market demands shift
- Young workers should prioritize developing strategic thinking skills and learning to guide AI systems, mirroring managerial and guidance roles
- Historical technology transitions (Industrial Revolution vs. electricity/IT) had opposite effects on skill premiums; AI's trajectory remains uncertain but speed of capability improvement is unprecedented
- Personalized learning powered by AI could represent one of the largest changes in educational capability in 100+ years

## Key Quotes

> "If we really unlock AI's capabilities for helping people learn, it could be much easier to switch between different professions. I'm really hopeful that we end up somewhere closer to a career lattice that works for workers as opposed to a career ladder where there's much more risk."

> "Whether you're more automated or augmented really depends on what are the tasks that you're focusing on. Are you increasing the scope of tasks that you can do, or are your tasks getting shrunk by the introduction of this technology?"

> "The ability and the potential to be someone like Ramanujan or at least creative in a productive way, I think it resides in us all."
