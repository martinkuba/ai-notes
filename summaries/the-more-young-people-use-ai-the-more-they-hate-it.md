# The more young people use AI, the more they hate it

**Original source:** [The more young people use AI, the more they hate it](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/920401/gen-z-ai)
**Author:** Janus Rose

## Summary

Despite being early adopters of AI chatbots, Gen Z is experiencing a significant cultural backlash against the technology, with their optimism hitting record lows. Recent polling shows only 18 percent of Gen Z now express hopefulness about AI (down from 27 percent), and nearly 50 percent believe risks outweigh benefits. Young people face a contradictory pressure: they're told AI will eliminate jobs while simultaneously being mandated to use these tools to remain competitive. This contradiction is particularly acute in educational settings, where universities are rapidly integrating AI into curricula through partnerships with companies like OpenAI, often without clear pedagogical justification.

Gen Z's skepticism stems from multiple concerns: fears of cognitive offloading and diminished critical thinking abilities (supported by neuroscience research), environmental and social harms, the prevalence of AI-generated content, and uncertainty about job market impacts. Many young people acknowledge regularly using chatbots while simultaneously resenting the technology and actively avoiding it when possible. Research indicates 74 percent use chatbots monthly, yet 65 percent believe the tools prevent critical engagement with ideas. The generational frustration reflects broader awareness of AI's limitations—from hallucinations to emotional manipulation—making Gen Z more realistic than tech company evangelists who promote AI as a universal solution.

## Main Ideas

- Gen Z's hopefulness about AI has dropped dramatically: only 18% express hope and 22% feel excited, despite widespread adoption of these tools
- Young people face contradictory pressures: warnings that AI will eliminate jobs while being told they must use AI to remain employable and competitive
- Universities are adopting "integrate first, find use cases later" approaches, integrating AI into curricula without demonstrating clear educational value
- Research shows cognitive offloading through AI use reduces brain activity and critical thinking skills, diminishing skepticism and ability to discern truth
- Gen Z demonstrates sophisticated understanding of AI limitations (hallucinations, unreliability) and resists hype from tech CEOs compared to older generations
- AI use has become culturally toxic and socially stigmatized among young people, creating shame around adoption despite practical use
- Concerns extend beyond personal impact to systemic issues: environmental damage, misinformation, academic integrity, and effects on human relationships and communication skills
