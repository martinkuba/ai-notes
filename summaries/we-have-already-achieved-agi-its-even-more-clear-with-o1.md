# "We have already achieved AGI. It's even more clear with O1"

**Original source:** ["We have already achieved AGI. It's even more clear with O1": OpenAI employee claims 'o1' constitutes AGI](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/we-have-already-achieved-agi-openai-employee-claims)
**Author:** Kevin Okemwa

## Summary

This article reports on a claim by OpenAI technical employee Vahid Kazemi that the company has already achieved AGI (artificial general intelligence) with the release of the o1 reasoning model. Kazemi posted on X that while OpenAI's models haven't achieved "better than any human at any task," they are "better than most humans at most tasks." He argued that the scientific method itself can be summarized as a recipe — observe, hypothesize, verify — and that there's nothing that can't be learned with examples, pushing back against the common criticism that LLMs merely follow recipes.

The claim arrives in the context of several related developments at OpenAI. CEO Sam Altman had previously stated that superintelligence is only "a few thousand days away" and suggested AGI could whoosh by with "surprisingly little" societal impact. The article notes that OpenAI was reportedly in discussions to scrap a contractual clause that would void its Microsoft partnership upon achieving AGI — a move some speculated was designed to secure future Microsoft investments. The piece also covers the broader skepticism around these claims, including concerns about AI hype fading, OpenAI's potential $5 billion losses, and Elon Musk's lawsuit alleging OpenAI betrayed its founding mission.

The article highlights the fundamental tension in defining AGI: there's no consensus on what constitutes it. Kazemi's definition — better than most humans at most tasks — is far more permissive than the traditional definition of surpassing all human cognitive capabilities. This ambiguity in definition makes it possible for OpenAI insiders to claim AGI achievement while critics point out that current models still fail at many tasks humans find trivial.
