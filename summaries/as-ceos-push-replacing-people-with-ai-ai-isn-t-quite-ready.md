# As CEOs push replacing people with AI, AI isn't quite ready to do the job

**Original source:** [As CEOs push replacing people with AI, AI isn't quite ready to do the job](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/30/ai-jobs-replace-humans-ceos-amodei)
**Author:** Scott Rosenberg

## Summary

Businesses are racing to replace human workers with AI despite the technology not yet being reliable or capable enough to fully do so. CEOs are gambling that Silicon Valley will improve AI fast enough that they can rush through workforce cutbacks today without getting caught short-handed. While AI tools can enhance productivity, they generally aren't adept, independent, or reliable enough to fully replace office workers—but AI leaders like Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warn this shift is imminent, predicting AI could wipe out half of entry-level white-collar jobs and spike unemployment to 10-20% within one to five years.

Several companies that made early high-profile announcements about replacing workers with AI have already reversed course. Klarna, which set out to be "OpenAI's favorite guinea pig," had to rehire support workers because customers wanted real human interaction. IBM predicted in 2023 it would replace 8,000 jobs with AI but two years later had only replaced a couple hundred HR employees while increasing hiring in other areas. These examples suggest the transition is messier and slower than headlines imply.

Economists remain divided on the severity of AI's impact. Some point to previous waves of digital change that arrived with devastating predictions that didn't pan out. Others argue AI represents a fundamentally different kind of technology that could hit too fast for adaptation. Meanwhile, unemployment among recent college grads is growing faster than other groups—an early warning sign. The political dimensions are volatile: Trump's populist MAGA base is likely to resist AI-driven change even as the president's tech allies push for more investment.

## Main Ideas

- CEOs are betting on AI improvements fast enough to justify workforce cutbacks today, despite current AI limitations
- Companies like Klarna and IBM that rushed to replace workers with AI have already had to reverse course
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warns of potential 10-20% unemployment from AI within 1-5 years
- Rising unemployment among recent college graduates (6% vs 4% overall) may be an early signal of AI's labor market impact
- Historical precedent suggests technological transformations create more jobs long-term, but AI's speed may prevent adaptation
- Americans broadly want AI introduced with more care and less haste, per polling data
