# A Critical Question in Agent Design

**Original source:** [A critical question in agent design is "how do we..."](https://x.com/emollick/status/2052066205226123472/?s=12&t=lja9AMD11WXSlUvoMkbJMw&rw_tt_thread=True)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Mollick raises a fundamental challenge in agent design: the need to intentionally create space for meaningful human decision-making within agentic workflows. Rather than treating automation as a complete replacement for human involvement, he argues that effective systems should preserve moments where humans are given significant, interesting, or variance-producing decisions to make as work unfolds. This perspective shifts the design goal from maximal automation to strategic augmentation.

A second dimension of his argument addresses competitive advantage in an era of AI commoditization. When multiple companies rely on the same language models like Claude, the model itself becomes a commodity rather than a source of differentiation. Mollick contends that sustainable competitive advantage comes not from automating entire workflows, but from reimagining how work is structured to integrate human and AI capabilities in complementary ways. The true moat lies in the reimagined process architecture, not in the automation technology itself.

## Main Ideas

- **Intentional human decision-making**: Agentic workflows should preserve meaningful decision points for humans rather than seeking complete automation
- **Meaningful variance**: Effective designs give humans decisions that matter and produce variance in outcomes
- **Model commoditization**: When all companies use identical AI models, the model cannot be a source of competitive advantage
- **Augmentation over replacement**: Sustainable advantage comes from AI-human collaboration rather than human substitution
- **Process innovation as moat**: Competitive differentiation derives from reimagined workflows that leverage both AI and human strengths

## Key Quotes

- "How do we build agentic workflows so humans are given significant, interesting, or variance-producing decisions as they come up in the work?"
- "A Claude-run company has no source of competitive advantage compared to other Claude-run firms."
- "Automating entire workflows will likely prove less of a sustainable moat than reimagining them to include humans in key ways, where AIs and humans augment each other."
