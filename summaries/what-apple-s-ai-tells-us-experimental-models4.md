# What Apple's AI Tells Us: Experimental Models⁴

**Original source:** [What Apple's AI Tells Us: Experimental Models⁴](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/what-apples-ai-tells-us-experimental)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Ethan Mollick uses Apple's AI ("Apple Intelligence") launch as a lens to examine four kinds of experimentation happening across the AI industry: AI models, models of use, business models, and mental models of the future. On AI models, he notes that frontier models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Claude 3 Opus) consistently outperform specialized models — even GPT-4 beat BloombergGPT on finance tasks without specialized training. Apple lacks a frontier model but has built small on-device models comparable to Mistral 7b and a cloud model slightly better than the original ChatGPT, partnering with OpenAI for questions too hard for its own models.

On models of use, Mollick contrasts Apple's narrow, practical approach (Siri doing specific tasks like "send this photo after making the colors pop") with frontier models' open-ended, Swiss-army-knife capabilities (like having Gemini analyze comics based on your email history). Both approaches have merits: narrow AI is more intuitive and predictable, while frontier models offer higher potential upside along with frustrating inconsistency. Microsoft's Copilot takes a middle path with "it just works" integration into Office apps, but at the cost of constrained capabilities.

On business models, everyone charges approximately $20/month for premium AI access while also offering some free tiers, but sustainable monetization remains unclear. Apple differentiates strongly on trust and privacy, with on-device processing, encrypted cloud handling, and instant deletion of data. On mental models of the future, Mollick identifies a key philosophical divide: OpenAI and Anthropic are explicitly building toward AGI (making current systems mere stepping stones), while Apple is building practical, narrow AI that works today. This AGI-oriented mindset explains why OpenAI's products may feel unpolished — the company may not invest heavily in refining systems it expects to be obsolete soon.

## Main Ideas

- Frontier models consistently outperform specialized models, even in the specialists' own domains
- Apple uses small on-device models for simple tasks and partners with OpenAI for complex ones
- Narrow AI (Apple/Copilot) trades capability ceiling for usability; frontier models trade consistency for power
- AI business models are still experimental — the ~$20/month price point is an industry convention, not a proven model
- Apple differentiates on privacy with on-device processing, encrypted cloud handling, and data deletion
- The AGI-focused mindset of OpenAI/Anthropic shapes their product decisions — current products are stepping stones, not destinations
- Having companies take many different approaches will likely accelerate overall AI adoption
