# The economist who was terrified of AI just found a rare reason for hope

**Original source:** [The economist who was terrified of AI just found a rare reason for hope](https://fortune.com/2026/04/19/alex-imas-human-jobs-ai-economy-chicago-economist-substack-doomsday-scenario/)
**Author:** Nick Lichtenberg

## Summary

Alex Imas, a prominent behavioral economist at the University of Chicago, has shifted from warning about catastrophic AI-driven job displacement to proposing a more optimistic framework for how the economy might adapt. His argument draws on structural change theory—the same economic principle that explained how farming declined from 40% of the workforce in 1900 to under 2% today without economic collapse. As AI commoditizes routine work, Imas argues that demand and employment will migrate toward the "relational sector"—jobs requiring genuine human connection and interaction such as nursing, teaching, childcare, and hospitality work.

The core insight is rooted in behavioral economics and mimetic desire theory: people derive value not just from functional utility but from scarcity and human interaction. His research shows consumers willingly pay double for identical goods when they're exclusive, and that AI involvement in production undermines this premium because AI-generated goods seem infinitely reproducible. As AI makes commodities cheaper and more abundant, truly scarce goods—human presence, social connection, and emotional support—will become economically valuable. Imas cites Starbucks' recent decision to reverse automation and rehire workers as evidence that human-centric services command premium pricing.

However, Imas emphasizes that this optimistic transition depends entirely on speed. Gradual change allows labor markets to retrain and adapt, but rapid automation could still trigger the demand-collapse scenario he previously feared. The critical uncertainty remains whether institutions can reallocate workers fast enough to prevent widespread unemployment and economic contraction.

## Main Ideas

- **Structural economic transformation**: As AI makes commodity production cheaper, employment will shift toward "relational sectors" requiring human interaction, mirroring historical transitions from agriculture to manufacturing to services
- **Mimetic desire and scarcity economics**: Humans value goods partly because others want them and cannot have them; AI-generated products undermine this premium because they appear infinitely reproducible
- **The relational sector as solution**: Jobs in nursing, teaching, therapy, childcare, and hospitality—already employing 50 million Americans—will become core economic value drivers as routine tasks are automated
- **Evidence from wealth behavior**: Billionaires with unlimited resources primarily spend time on relational goods (podcasts, social interaction, live performances) rather than consuming unlimited entertainment alone
- **The speed problem**: The theory only works if transition occurs gradually; rapid AI acceleration could still trigger unemployment and demand collapse
- **Institutional barriers real-world**: Beyond technology capability, legal review, bureaucracy, and institutional gridlock significantly slow AI deployment in sectors like healthcare
- **Job transformation, not elimination**: Many existing jobs won't disappear but will evolve to emphasize their irreducible human components as routine work is automated
