# AGI is coming faster than we think — we must get ready now

**Original source:** [AGI is coming faster than we think — we must get ready now](https://venturebeat.com/ai/agi-is-coming-faster-than-we-think-we-must-get-ready-now/)
**Author:** Gary Grossman

## Summary

Leading AI figures including Anthropic's Dario Amodei, OpenAI's Sam Altman, and Elon Musk have put forward aggressive timelines for the arrival of artificial general intelligence (AGI) or superintelligence, ranging from as early as 2026 to around 2029-2034. Amodei defines powerful AI as systems smarter than Nobel Prize winners across most relevant fields, while Altman suggests superintelligence could arrive "in a few thousand days." These predictions align with futurist Ray Kurzweil's longstanding forecast of AGI by 2029, a prediction he first made in 1995.

The article explores the profound implications of these timelines. On the upside, powerful AI could yield cures for cancer and depression, achieve fusion energy, and usher in an era of abundance. On the downside, the risks include vast unemployment, income inequality, and autonomous weapons. In the near term, MIT's Andrew McAfee sees AI as enhancing rather than replacing jobs, providing "an army of clerks, colleagues and coaches," though Musk has suggested that ultimately "none of us will have a job."

However, not everyone shares this optimistic timeline. Deep learning skeptic Gary Marcus argues current AI lacks the deep reasoning skills needed for AGI, and Linus Torvalds has described AI as "90% marketing and 10% reality." A new OpenAI benchmark called SimpleQA demonstrated that even leading frontier models like GPT-4o and o1 struggle with simple factual questions, with the best performer still producing incorrect answers half the time. The article concludes by invoking Amara's Law -- we tend to overestimate short-term impact while underestimating long-term potential -- and argues that whatever the exact timeline, the gap between current capabilities and true AGI provides valuable time to develop safety frameworks and prepare institutions.

## Main Ideas

- Multiple leading AI figures (Amodei, Altman, Musk, Kurzweil) predict AGI or superintelligence arriving between 2026 and 2034.
- The potential benefits of AGI include medical breakthroughs, fusion energy, and new opportunities for creativity, while risks include mass unemployment, inequality, and autonomous weapons.
- Skeptics like Gary Marcus and Linus Torvalds argue current AI technologies are overhyped and lack the reasoning capabilities needed for true AGI.
- OpenAI's SimpleQA benchmark reveals that even the best frontier models fail on half of simple factual questions, highlighting the gap between current capabilities and AGI.
- Amara's Law suggests we may overestimate AI's short-term impact while underestimating its long-term transformative potential.
- The current gap between AI capabilities and AGI provides crucial time for developing safety frameworks and institutional readiness.
