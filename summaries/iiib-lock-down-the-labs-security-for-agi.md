# IIIb. Lock Down the Labs: Security for AGI

**Original source:** [IIIb. Lock Down the Labs: Security for AGI](https://situational-awareness.ai/lock-down-the-labs/)
**Author:** SITUATIONAL AWARENESS - The Decade Ahead

## Summary

This essay argues that America's leading AI labs have catastrophically inadequate security, effectively handing the blueprints for AGI to adversaries like China on a silver platter. The author identifies two critical assets that must be protected: model weights (the finished AI model as a file) and algorithmic secrets (the key technical breakthroughs needed to build AGI). While weight security will matter most once AGI is built, algorithmic secrets are already at acute risk today, as labs are actively developing the paradigm-breaking techniques (RL, self-play, synthetic data) that will take AI systems past the data wall and toward AGI.

The essay catalogs the formidable capabilities of state-level adversaries -- from zero-click iPhone hacks to infiltrating air-gapped nuclear programs -- and argues that current AI lab security is laughably insufficient against such threats. Labs operate with "random startup security" while developing what the author calls the nation's most important defense secrets. The author warns that failing to protect algorithmic secrets in the next 12-24 months could irreversibly supply China with the key breakthroughs for AGI, making export controls on compute largely moot since algorithmic advantages can be worth 10x-100x more compute.

The path to adequate security requires government involvement and would ultimately entail air-gapped datacenters with military-grade physical security, research personnel working from SCIFs, extreme personnel vetting, multi-key signoff for code execution, NSA-level penetration testing, and rigid information compartmentalization. The essay draws a historical parallel to the atomic era, noting how Leo Szilard's last-minute secrecy appeals about graphite moderator measurements may have doomed the German nuclear program by denying them a critical correction. The author argues that a similar window exists now for AI, but is closing rapidly.

## Main Ideas

- AI labs currently have "startup-level" security that is entirely inadequate against state-level espionage, despite developing what will become the most consequential national defense secrets.
- Two critical assets must be protected: model weights (especially important near AGI) and algorithmic secrets (critical right now, as key AGI breakthroughs are being developed).
- Algorithmic secrets may be worth 10x-100x more than compute advantages, making their protection potentially more important than chip export controls.
- The worst-case scenario is China stealing automated-AI-researcher model weights on the cusp of an intelligence explosion, instantly closing the gap and triggering an existential race with no margin for safety.
- Achieving state-actor-proof security will require government partnership: air-gapped clusters, SCIFs, security clearances, rigid compartmentalization, and NSA-level pen-testing.
- Historical parallel: Szilard's secrecy campaign about graphite measurements may have doomed Germany's nuclear program -- a similar opportunity exists now for AI if labs act quickly.
- The failure window is narrow: key AGI algorithmic breakthroughs will likely leak to adversaries within 12-24 months without dramatic security improvements.

## Key Quotes

- "On the current course, the leading Chinese AGI labs won't be in Beijing or Shanghai -- they'll be in San Francisco and London."
- "AI lab security isn't much better than 'random startup security.' Directly selling the AGI secrets to the CCP would at least be more honest."
- "We're developing the most powerful weapon mankind has ever created... And yet AI lab security is probably worse than a random defense contractor making bolts."
