# AI And The Quantity And Quality Of Creative Products: Have LLMs Boosted Creation Of Valuable Books?

**Original source:** [AI And The Quantity And Quality Of Creative Products: Have Llms Boosted Creation Of Valuable Books?](https://readwise.io/reader/document_raw_content/451209106)
**Author:** Imke Reimers, Joel Waldfogel

## Summary

This NBER working paper investigates how large language models (LLMs) have affected book publishing between 2022 and 2025. The authors find that new book releases tripled during this period, driven primarily by AI-enabled authors entering the market. Using Amazon data and AI detection tools, they document that while average book quality declined as measured by reader engagement (number of ratings), the sheer volume increase resulted in more moderately high-quality books reaching consumers. AI-containing books substantially underperform human-authored books in usage and ratings, with detected AI books receiving 61% fewer ratings on average. However, the human-AI quality gap narrows over time as authors and AI technology improve.

The welfare analysis reveals that AI books increased consumer surplus by 7.23% in 2025, a modest gain relative to the tripling of book supply. Crucially, the majority of the quality differential between AI and human-authored books stems from author selection—lower-quality and inexperienced authors disproportionately adopt AI—rather than AI inherently reducing quality. The influx of AI books has not displaced incumbent authors; those active before LLMs increased their output during the AI era. The findings suggest AI primarily expands the long tail of creative content rather than improving the quality frontier.

## Main Ideas

- **Dramatic supply increase**: New ebook releases nearly tripled from ~100,000 monthly titles in 2022 to over 300,000 in late 2025, tracking closely with LLM diffusion
- **Quality declines but diversity increases**: Average book quality declined while the absolute number of moderately high-quality releases increased, particularly outside the top 200 titles per category-month
- **AI books are lower quality**: AI-detected books receive 61% fewer ratings and lower star ratings than human-authored books, but this gap shrinks from 2023-2025
- **Author selection explains most quality gap**: Approximately 65% of the human-AI quality differential reflects low-quality authors adopting AI rather than AI reducing inherent writing quality
- **Modest positive welfare effects**: AI books added 0.44% consumer surplus in 2023, 3.26% in 2024, and 7.23% in 2025—small relative to supply growth
- **No displacement of incumbents**: Authors active before 2023 increased their output during the LLM era despite market flooding with AI-generated content
- **Category variation**: AI adoption and growth are concentrated in nonfiction categories (travel, self-help, technology) rather than fiction or literary genres

## Key Quotes

"The LLM influx has delivered some books in the middle range of the usage/quality distribution, and the LLM-era entry process delivered seven percent more consumer surplus from books than the pre-LLM process in 2025."

"Most of the AI-human quality gap stems from low-quality authors' adoption of AI, but the AI-human gap shrinks between 2023 and 2025."

"While we document that AI facilitates the creation of content that is valuable to consumers, there is a separate question of whether AI facilitates the creation of content that would be well-regarded by critics or other cultural elites."
