# OpenAI Says New York Times Lawsuit Against It Is 'Without Merit'

**Original source:** [OpenAI Says New York Times Lawsuit Against It Is 'Without Merit'](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/08/technology/openai-new-york-times-lawsuit.html)
**Author:** Cade Metz

## Summary

In January 2024, OpenAI responded to the New York Times' copyright infringement lawsuit by publishing a blog post calling the suit "without merit." The Times had sued OpenAI and Microsoft in late December 2023, alleging that millions of its articles were used to train AI technologies like ChatGPT without authorization, and that these chatbots now compete with the newspaper as a source of reliable information. OpenAI countered by asserting that training on copyrighted material constitutes fair use under the law and pointed to its partnerships with other news organizations, including the Associated Press.

OpenAI revealed that it had been in partnership discussions with the Times, with the last communication occurring on December 19 — just eight days before the lawsuit was filed. During negotiations, the Times had raised concerns about ChatGPT "regurgitating" near-verbatim excerpts of its articles but declined to provide specific examples. OpenAI characterized this verbatim reproduction as a "rare bug" it was working to fix and stated that intentionally manipulating models to produce such output violates its terms of use.

The lawsuit was the first major copyright suit filed by an American media organization against an AI company, though other groups including novelists and computer programmers had already filed similar suits. The case sits at the center of a broader legal and economic debate sparked by generative AI: whether the massive quantities of online content used to train these systems — stories, artwork, photos, message board posts — represent significant untapped value that content creators deserve to be compensated for.

## Main Ideas

- OpenAI called the New York Times' copyright lawsuit "without merit," claiming fair use protections for training AI on copyrighted content.
- The Times was the first major American media organization to sue an AI company over copyright, though similar suits from authors and programmers preceded it.
- OpenAI acknowledged that ChatGPT sometimes reproduces near-verbatim article excerpts but characterized it as a "rare bug," not intended behavior.
- Partnership negotiations between OpenAI and the Times broke down shortly before the lawsuit, with OpenAI expressing surprise at the legal action.
- The case highlights a fundamental tension in generative AI: online content used for training may have significant untapped economic value that creators are not being compensated for.

## Key Quotes

- "Intentionally manipulating our models to regurgitate is not an appropriate use of our technology and is against our terms of use." — OpenAI
- "We look forward to continued collaboration with news organizations, helping elevate their ability to produce quality journalism by realizing the transformative potential of A.I." — OpenAI
