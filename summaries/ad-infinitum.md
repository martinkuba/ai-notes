# Ad Infinitum

**Original source:** [Ad Infinitum](https://matthiasott.com/notes/ad-infinitum)
**Author:** Matthias Ott

## Summary

Google's 2026 I/O announcement of radically reimagined search—replacing traditional blue links with generative UI, intelligent search boxes, information agents, and the personal AI assistant Spark—represents a fundamental shift in value extraction. Notably, despite advertising generating $295 billion in 2025 revenue, the keynote made no mention of monetization. However, research from Google's own labs reveals a hidden infrastructure: novel auction mechanisms like "token auctions" (where advertisers bid on individual words the model generates) and "prominence allocation" (where auctions determine how enthusiastically each product is mentioned in AI responses) embed ads directly into LLM-generated output, making them indistinguishable from answers.

The deeper shift unravels the original web contract that sustained Google's dominance for 25 years: freely published content indexed and linked back to, driving traffic to sources. Generative UI absorbs content directly into synthesized answers without linking back. The same logic applies to ads—there are no discrete slots to monetize anymore. Instead, ads become the answer itself. Spark's demands for personal data collection (emails, calendar, purchase history, files) provide the targeting context these auction systems need to operate at scale. Google's advertisers now lose creative and keyword control entirely, handing decisions to Google's algorithms.

The contrast between Google's I/O messaging (helpfulness, trust, productivity) and the advertiser-focused Marketing Live event (same week, different audience) reveals the strategy. There, Google stated plainly: "The best ads must be answers." Ads in AI Mode are generated by Gemini to read as conversation; advertisers must surrender creative control to Google's system. Paid placements appear as list items in recommendations, framed as objective guidance. The infrastructure being built is a monetization engine disguised as assistance, dependent on users accepting deep surveillance.

## Main Ideas

- Google's new generative search is fundamentally an advertising infrastructure, though omitted from public messaging—ads are embedded into AI-generated content rather than appearing as discrete slots
- The traditional web contract (index free content, link back to sources) is being replaced with direct synthesis into Google's proprietary answers, eliminating referral traffic
- Novel auction mechanisms allow advertisers to bid on prominence and tone within AI responses, making organic and paid content indistinguishable to users
- Google's personal AI agent Spark collects intimate user data (emails, calendar, purchases) to enable hyper-targeted placement in auction systems
- Advertisers lose keyword and creative control; Google's algorithms decide if and how products are presented, framed as neutral advice
- The shift from open web to closed proprietary platform enables granular monetization of user attention without visible advertising

## Key Quotes

- "The ad isn't *next to* the answer. The ad *is* the answer. Or rather, it shapes how much space and enthusiasm each product gets within the answer."

- "You can't choose keywords anymore."

- "Google is betting its entire future on a world where people eagerly hand over their emails, their files, their habits, and their trust – to an AI system that will quietly auction off their attention, word by word, to the highest bidder."
