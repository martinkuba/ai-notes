# Making AI Work: Leadership, Lab, and Crowd

**Original source:** [Making AI Work: Leadership, Lab, and Crowd](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/making-ai-work-leadership-lab-and)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Individual AI users report massive productivity gains—workers claim AI halves their time on 41% of tasks or triples overall productivity. Yet organizations report only modest gains from AI deployment. This disconnect reveals a critical insight: individual performance improvements don't automatically translate to organizational gains. Organizations need intentional innovation in incentives, processes, and work structure to capture these benefits.

Mollick proposes a three-part framework for AI transformation. **Leadership** must articulate a vivid vision of the AI-powered future, clarify how work will change, and create psychological safety and incentives for employees to experiment openly with AI. **The Crowd**—the bulk of the workforce—are the real innovators, discovering practical AI applications, but many hide their use due to fear of layoffs, punishment, or loss of status. **The Lab** is a focused team that rapidly prototypes solutions from crowd discoveries, builds organization-specific AI benchmarks, experiments with bleeding-edge capabilities, and creates provocative demos to shift organizational imagination.

Beyond implementing these three elements, organizations must fundamentally rethink their structures and assumptions about work. When capabilities once requiring weeks now require minutes, the bottleneck shifts from execution to decision-making. Organizations must adapt continuously as AI capabilities evolve rapidly—the advantage goes to those who learn fastest.

## Main Ideas

- Individual AI productivity gains require organizational innovation to become company-wide performance improvements; training and access alone are insufficient
- Workers actively hide AI use due to fear of layoffs, punishment, or being held to higher standards—addressing these incentive problems is essential
- Organizations need three interconnected components: leadership vision and psychological safety, decentralized crowd-driven innovation, and a Lab function for rapid experimentation
- Organization-specific AI benchmarks are more valuable than generic ones; companies should test AI on actual work tasks rather than relying on published benchmarks
- Fundamental organizational structures, processes, and assumptions about work were designed around human intelligence and require rethinking for an AI-enabled world
- The Lab should focus on rapid iteration and building prototypes that don't work "yet," preparing for the next generation of AI capabilities rather than optimizing current ones
- Sustained organizational learning and faster adaptation than competitors—not technology implementation—determine competitive advantage in AI adoption

## Key Quotes

- "We are all figuring this out together. So, if you want to gain an advantage, you are going to have to figure it out faster than everyone else."
- "When research that once took weeks now takes minutes, the bottleneck isn't the research anymore, it's figuring out what research to do. When code can be written quickly, the limitation isn't programming speed, it's understanding what to build."
- "The truth is that even this framework might not be enough. Our organizations, from their structures to their processes to their goals, were all built around human intelligence because that's all we had."
