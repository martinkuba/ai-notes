# 'The Godfather of A.I.' Leaves Google and Warns of Danger Ahead

**Original source:** ['The Godfather of A.I.' Leaves Google and Warns of Danger Ahead](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/05/01/technology/ai-google-chatbot-engineer-quits-hinton.html)
**Author:** Cade Metz

## Summary

Geoffrey Hinton, the 75-year-old British-Canadian computer scientist widely known as "the Godfather of AI," resigned from Google after more than a decade to speak freely about the dangers of artificial intelligence. Hinton's 2012 breakthrough with students Ilya Sutskever and Alex Krishevsky -- a neural network that could teach itself to identify objects in photos -- became the intellectual foundation for modern AI systems including ChatGPT and Google Bard, and earned him the Turing Award in 2018.

Hinton's concerns mark a significant shift in his own thinking. He initially believed AI systems were inferior to the human brain, but after witnessing Google and OpenAI build systems with much larger datasets, he changed his view, suggesting "what is going on in these systems is actually a lot better than what is going on in the brain." His immediate concern is that the internet will be flooded with false content, making it impossible for average people to discern truth. He also worries about job displacement (chatbots replacing paralegals, translators, and other rote-task workers) and, longer-term, the threat of truly autonomous systems that learn unexpected behaviors from their training data. Hinton sees the competitive race between Google and Microsoft as potentially unstoppable, and unlike nuclear weapons, AI development can happen in secret, making global regulation extremely difficult. He believes the world's leading scientists must collaborate to find control mechanisms.

## Main Ideas

- Geoffrey Hinton, whose neural network research underpins modern AI, resigned from Google to warn publicly about AI risks
- Hinton now believes AI systems may already process information better than the human brain in some respects
- His most immediate concern is AI-generated misinformation flooding the internet and eroding the ability to discern truth
- Job displacement across white-collar roles (paralegals, translators, assistants) is a medium-term risk
- The competitive race between tech giants creates pressure that may be impossible to stop without global regulation
- Unlike nuclear weapons, there's no way to verify whether companies or countries are secretly developing AI, complicating governance

## Key Quotes

- "Maybe what is going on in these systems is actually a lot better than what is going on in the brain."
- "It is hard to see how you can prevent the bad actors from using it for bad things."
- "The idea that this stuff could actually get smarter than people -- a few people believed that. But most people thought it was way off. I thought it was 30 to 50 years or even longer away. Obviously, I no longer think that."
