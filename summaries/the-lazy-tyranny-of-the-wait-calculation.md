# The Lazy Tyranny of the Wait Calculation

**Original source:** [The Lazy Tyranny of the Wait Calculation](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-lazy-tyranny-of-the-wait-calculation)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Ethan Mollick applies the concept of a "Wait Calculation" -- originally used for interstellar travel -- to AI-era project planning. The Wait Calculation asks: when technology improves rapidly, should you start a project now or wait for better tools? Just as launching a 12,000-year interstellar voyage today would be pointless when fusion drives could make the trip in fifty years, starting certain projects today may be futile when AI capabilities are advancing at better-than-exponential rates.

Mollick illustrates with his own experience: a board game for innovation (The Breakthrough Game) that took years to develop was effectively replaced by a GPT that generates dozens of ideas in minutes, and elaborate teaching simulation software at Wharton Interactive was compressed into simple AI prompts. He proposes a practical framework for the Wait Calculation: estimate project duration, project how advanced AI might be by completion, calculate time savings from improved AI, and assess whether AI might make the project obsolete or fundamentally shift its meaning. However, Mollick warns against taking Wait Calculations too seriously, identifying two traps: the learning trap (waiting robs you of the expertise needed to eventually work with AI effectively) and the incentive trap (perpetual waiting destroys motivation). He notes that prediction markets place "weak AGI" within a few years, meaning the timeline for academic papers or software projects may exceed the timeline to major AI capability jumps. The key takeaway is not to stop working, but to factor AI's trajectory into long-term project decisions.

## Main Ideas

- The "Wait Calculation" asks whether waiting for better AI tools would save more time than starting a project now
- Real-world examples: a years-long board game project and custom teaching software were both compressed to simple AI prompts
- Key factors: project duration, expected AI advancement, time savings from future AI, and whether the project's meaning shifts
- Two dangers of waiting: the learning trap (you need experience to effectively use AI later) and the incentive trap (perpetual deferral)
- Prediction markets suggest weak AGI within a few years, making many multi-year projects potentially obsolete before completion
- The Wait Calculation shouldn't prevent action but should inform how you approach and plan long-term work
