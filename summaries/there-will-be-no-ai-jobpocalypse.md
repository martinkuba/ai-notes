# There will be no AI jobpocalypse

**Original source:** [There will be no AI jobpocalypse](https://x.com/andrewyng/status/2054236506756370865/?s=12&t=lja9AMD11WXSlUvoMkbJMw&rw_tt_thread=True)

**Author:** Andrew Ng

## Summary

Andrew Ng argues that widespread fears of an AI-driven jobpocalypse are overblown and irresponsible, despite gaining traction in mainstream discourse. He points to concrete evidence that contradicts this narrative: software engineering, the sector most affected by AI tools, continues to show strong hiring; the U.S. unemployment rate remains healthy at 4.3%; and historical patterns demonstrate that technological waves create more jobs than they destroy. Ng acknowledges that AI is genuinely disrupting work and causing real stress for affected workers, but he distinguishes this from a collapse in the overall job market.

Ng identifies multiple incentive structures that fuel the jobpocalypse narrative. Frontier AI labs benefit from portraying AI as transformative and powerful (even promoting extinction scenarios), which justifies high valuations. AI companies anchor their pricing to displaced salaries ($100,000+) rather than traditional SaaS rates ($100-$1,000), enabling vastly higher charges. Corporations blame AI for layoffs rather than admitting they overhired during the pandemic, which is better for their public image than acknowledging poor capital allocation. Together, these incentives create a self-reinforcing narrative despite weak evidence.

Drawing historical parallels to other societal misunderstandings—nuclear power safety fears, population bomb anxieties, dietary fat misconceptions—Ng argues that we tell ourselves persistent false stories with real policy consequences. He predicts instead an "AI jobapalooza": expansion of specialized AI engineering roles, transformed (not eliminated) non-AI work, and greater job opportunities for those with AI proficiency. The key is preparing workers for different but plentiful future jobs.

## Main Ideas

- The AI jobpocalypse narrative is unfounded and potentially damaging, contradicted by strong hiring trends and healthy unemployment despite AI adoption
- AI, like prior technological waves, creates net job growth despite displacing certain roles; disruption is real but different from market collapse
- Multiple incentive structures propagate the jobpocalypse story: AI labs oversell power, companies charge premium prices based on salary anchoring, and corporations blame AI for strategic overhiring mistakes
- Societies have historically embraced persistent false narratives that drive poor policy decisions; the jobpocalypse risks becoming another
- The future will feature abundant but different employment opportunities, particularly in AI engineering roles, requiring workforce adaptation and AI skill development

## Key Quotes

- "Societies are capable of telling themselves stories for years that have little basis in reality and lead to poor society-wide decision making."
- "I predict the opposite: There will be an AI jobapalooza! AI will lead to a lot more good AI engineering jobs, and I'm also optimistic about the future of the overall job market."
- "To be clear, I recognize that AI is causing a lot of people's work to change. This is hard. This is stressful. (And to some, it can be fun.) I empathize with everyone affected. At the same time, this is very different from predicting a collapse of the job market."
