# A lot of problems with AI discourse are because "being..."

**Original source:** [A lot of problems with AI discourse are because "being..."](https://x.com/emollick/status/1977581271019856099/?s=20&rw_tt_thread=True)
**Author:** Ethan Mollick

## Summary

Ethan Mollick highlights a key finding from research suggesting that "being good at AI" -- referred to as "Theory of Mind" in the referenced paper -- appears to be a skill that is largely independent of domain expertise. This creates a disconnect in AI discourse: some highly skilled professionals gain significant value from AI tools while equally talented peers in the same field do not, and neither group understands the other's experience.

Mollick further notes that this skills gap is compounded by AI's "jagged" capabilities -- the technology excels in some areas while failing in others in unpredictable ways. This leads to a polarized discourse where some people overhype AI based on the domains where it performs well, while others underhype it based on domains where it falls short, sometimes with their own motivations for doing so. The combination of a hidden skill dimension and genuinely uneven AI performance makes productive conversation about AI's real value particularly difficult.

## Main Ideas

- The ability to effectively use AI appears to be an independent skill, separate from professional domain expertise.
- This creates a divide where equally skilled experts have vastly different experiences with AI, leading to mutual misunderstanding.
- AI has "jagged" abilities -- excelling unpredictably in some areas and failing in others -- which further complicates discourse.
- Both overhyping and underhyping of AI are driven by this combination of hidden skill variance and genuinely uneven capabilities.
