# One of the new, buzzy jobs in Silicon Valley is...

**Source:** [Andrew Ng on Twitter/X](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2061477558693384395/?rw_tt_thread=True)

## Summary

Andrew Ng analyzes the emerging AI Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role vs. the larger and more scalable AI Engineer role. FDEs — engineers embedded within client organizations to build and tune agentic workflows — are enjoying a resurgence as customization demand rises. The role was pioneered by Palantir for air-gapped government networks and requires both technical and business communication skills. However, Ng argues AI Engineer positions will vastly outnumber FDE slots: companies want more of their own employees owning AI projects, and vendor-neutral AI Engineers preserve the optionality to switch providers as the landscape evolves.

Ng sees surging near-term demand for AI Engineers fluent in LLM prompting, agentic frameworks, evals, and AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, etc.). He predicts the AI Engineer role will fragment into specializations over the coming decade — LLMOps Engineers, Evals Engineers, AI Data Engineers, Harness Engineers — mirroring how the generic Software Engineer role split into frontend, backend, mobile, DevOps, and data engineering.

## Main Ideas

- **FDE revival**: Demand driven by gap between off-the-shelf LLMs and production agentic workflows tailored to specific business needs.
- **AI Engineer >> FDE in volume**: Companies hire far more AI Engineers than FDEs; vendor lock-in risk makes FDEs less attractive.
- **Optionality premium**: In a fast-moving landscape, companies value the ability to swap AI vendors; FDEs reduce that optionality.
- **Specialization trajectory**: AI Engineering will fragment into specialized roles (LLMOps, Evals, Harness, Data) just as software engineering did before it.
- **Counter-narrative**: Rising demand for AI jobs refutes the jobpocalypse narrative — AI is creating new, well-compensated engineering careers.
