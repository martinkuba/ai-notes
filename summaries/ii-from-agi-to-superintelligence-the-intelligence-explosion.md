# II. From AGI to Superintelligence: the Intelligence Explosion

**Original source:** [II. From AGI to Superintelligence: the Intelligence Explosion](https://situational-awareness.ai/from-agi-to-superintelligence/)
**Author:** SITUATIONAL AWARENESS - The Decade Ahead

## Summary

This essay argues that once AGI is achieved, the transition to superintelligence could happen extraordinarily fast -- potentially within a single year -- driven by an "intelligence explosion" in which AGI systems automate AI research itself. The core mechanism is recursive: once AI systems reach the level of top AI researchers, we could run hundreds of millions of copies of them at 10x-100x human speed, generating an enormous increase in effective research effort. This automated research army could compress a decade's worth of algorithmic progress (approximately 5+ orders of magnitude of effective compute gains) into less than a year, producing systems vastly smarter than any human.

The author draws an analogy between the atomic bomb and the hydrogen bomb: AGI is like "The Bomb" (a more efficient version of what we know), while superintelligence is "The Super" (a qualitatively different and far more powerful force). The essay systematically addresses potential bottlenecks -- limited compute for experiments, complementarity with human researchers, diminishing returns to algorithmic progress, and the possibility that ideas get harder to find -- and argues none are sufficient to prevent a rapid intelligence explosion, though they may stretch the timeline from months to a few years.

The piece concludes by outlining the staggering consequences of superintelligence: solving robotics, dramatically accelerating all scientific and technological progress, triggering an industrial and economic explosion, providing decisive military advantages, and potentially enabling whoever controls it to overthrow existing power structures. The author frames this as one of the most volatile and dangerous periods in human history, drawing parallels to the early atomic era when scientists debated whether a nuclear chain reaction was possible.

## Main Ideas

- Once AGI is achieved, automating AI research could produce hundreds of millions of AI-researcher-equivalents running at 10x-100x human speed, compressing a decade of algorithmic progress into less than a year.
- The intelligence explosion does not require sci-fi breakthroughs -- it only needs to accelerate existing trendlines of algorithmic improvement (~0.5 OOMs/year).
- The most important bottleneck is limited compute for running experiments, but automated researchers would likely use compute at least 10x more effectively than humans through superior intuitions, fewer bugs, and better experiment selection.
- Complementarities and the "long tail" of automation may delay full automation by 1-2 years, but a proto-automated-researcher to full superintelligence pipeline could complete by the end of the decade.
- Superintelligence would solve robotics, dramatically accelerate science across all fields, enable decisive military advantage, and potentially allow whoever controls it to reshape global power structures.
- The transition period from AGI to superintelligence is framed as one of the most intense and dangerous moments in human history, analogous to the early nuclear era.
