# Judging by my tl there is a growing gap in...

**Original source:** [Judging by my tl there is a growing gap in...](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2042334451611693415/?s=20&rw_tt_thread=True)
**Author:** Andrej Karpathy

## Summary

Andrej Karpathy identifies a growing gap in public understanding of AI capabilities, driven by two factors. First, many people formed their views on AI by using free-tier or older/deprecated models (like ChatGPT's free tier or OpenAI's Advanced Voice Mode), which do not reflect the capabilities of the latest state-of-the-art agentic models such as OpenAI Codex and Claude Code. Viral videos of AI fumbling simple queries have reinforced dismissive attitudes that are out of date with current frontier capabilities.

Second, even among those paying for top-tier models, AI's most dramatic improvements are concentrated in "peaky" technical domains like programming, math, and research -- areas where reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (such as passing unit tests) has been most effective. Everyday use cases like search, writing, and advice have not seen comparable leaps, partly because these tasks are harder to evaluate with explicit reward functions and partly because they generate less revenue, so they receive less optimization focus from AI companies.

This creates two distinct groups talking past each other: casual users who see AI as flawed and overhyped, and professional technical users who witness frontier agentic models "melting" programming problems that would normally take days or weeks. Karpathy argues the latter group is experiencing a justified sense of alarm about the pace of capability improvements, while the former group is operating on stale impressions. The gap is widening as the two properties driving progress -- verifiable reward functions amenable to RL training, and high B2B value attracting engineering focus -- continue to accelerate technical domains specifically.

## Main Ideas

- There is a growing perception gap between casual AI users and professional technical users regarding AI capabilities.
- Many people's views are anchored to outdated or free-tier AI experiences that do not reflect current frontier models.
- The most dramatic AI improvements are concentrated in technical domains (coding, math, research) due to reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards.
- Everyday use cases (writing, search, advice) have improved less because they lack easy-to-verify reward signals and generate less revenue focus.
- Frontier agentic models like OpenAI Codex and Claude Code can now solve programming problems that previously took days or weeks of human effort.
- The two groups -- casual users and professional technical users -- are increasingly talking past each other about AI's true capabilities.
- Two key drivers of rapid progress in technical domains: verifiable reward functions for RL training, and high B2B value attracting engineering investment.
